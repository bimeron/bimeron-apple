## Material findings

1. **The “exact” quota contract is incomplete (README line 15).** Maxima match U, but the text omits the archive bound of 1–268,435,456 bytes, nonempty lower bounds for all three contents, checked arithmetic, exact actual byte-count enforcement and per-write aggregate accounting. U also requires one active operation per coordinator, one candidate and three files; T owns three/four ZIP-entry validation and the optional zero-byte directory. “Reject before allocation/write” must mean checking before each bounded allocation/write, including actual inflater output, rather than trusting a declared size or trying to know the complete archive outcome before decoding. Recommendation: list these invariants and distinguish T's archive checks from staging's content/write checks. Otherwise a fixture can pass the published maxima while violating U's contract.

2. **Publication and cleanup are underspecified (line 15).** U requires same-filesystem exclusive publication, no replacement/cross-volume fallback, a distinct published-but-unconfirmed outcome after a post-rename bookkeeping error, and cleanup that stops and reports `cleanupIncomplete` on identity mismatch, unexpected children or cleanup failure. “Cleans only the owned incomplete candidate” does not describe these failure cases. U also bounds write cancellation checks to at most 64 KiB; “bounded writes” leaves that obligation unspecified. Recommendation: add these requirements or clearly mark omitted failure handling as a prototype limitation. Do not allow a mock success/cancellation model to imply that real filesystem failure semantics have been validated.

3. **Evidence bounds and trust classification need an explicit contract (lines 11–13, 33).** The listed evidence fields and protected revalidation align with U. However, “bounded authenticated evidence” supplies neither concrete envelope limits nor the source of those limits, and the returned evidence list does not explicitly include a durable synthetic/untrusted classification. The mock disclaimer in prose is useful but must survive any candidate handoff/serialization. Recommendation: parent should establish byte/count limits for retained manifest/signature/context fields before making a total-memory claim, and require explicit trust classification in every mock result. Do not invent production limits in this prototype or treat an approval identity as approval itself.

## Test obligations for the parent

For the next completed-prototype review, provide small, bounded fixtures and inspectable results for:

- Ownership: mutate externally owned backing after the explicit snapshot and verify authenticated/retained bytes remain unchanged; separately test ordinary Data value mutation. Verify decoded contents cannot escape through a mutable alias. State exactly which initializer and backing variants were exercised.
- Whole-archive gate: valid binary followed by a bad/missing/duplicate notice or final entry-set failure must never produce a payload or start staging. A mock gate test validates control flow only; real ZIP parity remains T's obligation.
- Quotas: empty, exact-limit and limit-plus-one decisions, aggregate/overflow rejection, incorrect declared versus actual counts and operation concurrency. Use reduced injected limits or pure count checks for large boundaries; do not allocate production maxima here. Count-only tests do not prove real bounded inflation or actual maximum-size writes.
- Evidence: preserve exact original bytes, all identity/context fields and synthetic classification through handoff; missing, oversized or inconsistent mock evidence fails closed. Real signature, independent pin/approval and compatibility checks require separately authorized integration tests.
- Writes/publication: bounded chunks, short writes/EINTR, ENOSPC/write/close failures, cancellation before and after the exclusive rename, collision preserving existing data, EXDEV rejection and published-but-unconfirmed reporting. Fault injection can cover uncommon errors without host stress.
- Destination/cleanup: fixed names, exclusive no-follow creation, expected ownership/type/modes/link counts, ACL inheritance refusal, identity mismatch and unexpected-child cleanup refusal. Clearly report any mock filesystem coverage; do not claim hostile concurrent-race or protected-storage safety from sequential primitives.
- Memory reporting: record fixture sizes, simultaneously live owned buffers, archive release points, allocation-counter definition, measurement method and process RSS/peak separately. Attribute host statistics to the earlier observation; this review did not remeasure them. Compare strategies in sequential fresh subprocesses under the stated cap. Small samples cannot validate production maximum RSS or cancellation during T's inflater.

## Prototype simplifications versus production obligations

Synthetic contents, mock evidence, a mock archive-success gate, reduced quota fixtures and disposable staging are acceptable local research simplifications when labeled. They cannot establish ZIP acceptance, authentication, real allocation peaks, complete metadata/ACL enforcement, crash durability or S Prepared state. Any omitted filesystem error handling must remain visible as a limitation rather than a passed requirement.

Production still requires architecture agreement through main, T's independent acceptance/parity review, actual owned-byte/evidence integration, agreed evidence and peak-memory budgets, full destination/metadata policy, failure/reconciliation behavior and S's protected copy/revalidation contract. Maximum-size RSS benchmarking belongs to a separately approved suitable environment, not this capped shared-host experiment.
